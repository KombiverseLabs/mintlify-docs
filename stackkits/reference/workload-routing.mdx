Application Kit workloads — Immich, Cloudreve, Vaultwarden — deliver a single URL to their users. StackKits lets you declare more than one candidate route in your spec (for example, a local LAN hostname *and* a public hostname) and the compiler selects **exactly one** delivery route per workload. That selection is what the delivery adapter (Coolify, Komodo, or `standalone-compose`) then observes back after Apply.

This page explains how the selection works and how to reason about it.

## What a route is

A route is a declared way to reach one service on one workload. In `stack-spec.yaml` each route names its exposure, the workload service it fronts, the host and port, and its TLS profile.

Every route carries one of three **exposure** values:

| Exposure | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| `local` | Reachable only on the LAN / on-node network. Uses internal PKI. |
| `remote-private` | Reachable from a defined private mesh (for example, a VPN overlay). Not on the public internet. |
| `public` | Reachable from the public internet. Uses a public issuer. |

## How the compiler picks a route

For each Application Kit workload, StackKits looks at every declared route that targets that workload's module and entry service, and picks the **most externally exposed** one:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Filter to this workload">
    Only routes whose `moduleRef` matches the workload and whose `serviceRef` matches the workload's entry service are considered.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rank by exposure">
    Routes are ranked `public` &gt; `remote-private` &gt; `local`. The single highest-ranked route wins.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fail closed on ambiguity">
    If two or more routes tie at the highest rank — for example, two `public` routes on different hostnames — the compile is rejected. StackKits will not silently pick one; you must remove or reshape the duplicate so exactly one route wins.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  "Most externally exposed wins" is deliberate. It means adding a public route to a workload that previously had only a local route promotes the workload to that public route on the next compile — the local route stays reachable through the runtime, but the workload's declared, TLS-issued front door moves to the public one.
</Note>

## What the workload bundle freezes

Once the compiler picks the route, its identity is baked into the workload bundle handed to the delivery adapter. The frozen fields are:

- **Host and path** — the exact hostname and (if any) path prefix.
- **Port and target port** — the router port and the container port behind it.
- **Protocol** — HTTP or HTTPS on the router; the upstream protocol into the container.
- **TLS descriptor** — whether TLS is required, the mode, the minimum version, the certificate profile, and the issuer.
- **Route identity** — a stable `id`, `serviceRef`, `moduleRef`, and `exposure` used in evidence.

After Apply, the adapter must return exactly those fields in its readback. Coolify, Komodo, and `standalone-compose` all consume the same descriptor and are checked against it — no adapter can quietly rewrite the host or drop TLS.

## Workloads with no declared route

If you don't declare any route for a workload, that's supported — the workload is still delivered and remains reachable **through its adapter's internal network** (for example, on the StackKits routing network on the same node). It just advertises no StackKits-owned subdomain and gets no Traefik host/TLS labels.

Use this when a workload is only ever consumed by another service in the same deployment, or when you want to attach your own routing in front of it later.

## Example

```yaml stack-spec.yaml
services:
  immich:
    enabled: true

routes:
  # Local LAN route — internal PKI, LAN-only.
  - id: immich-local
    moduleRef: stackkits-immich-runtime
    serviceRef: immich-server
    exposure: local
    host: immich.home.lan
    port: 443
    targetPort: 2283
    tls:
      required: true
      mode: terminate
      profileRef: internal-pki

  # Public route — Let's Encrypt, reachable from anywhere.
  - id: immich-public
    moduleRef: stackkits-immich-runtime
    serviceRef: immich-server
    exposure: public
    host: photos.example.com
    port: 443
    targetPort: 2283
    tls:
      required: true
      mode: terminate
      issuerRef: letsencrypt
```

With both routes declared, the compiler picks `immich-public` (highest exposure) and freezes `photos.example.com` into the Immich workload bundle. Remove the `public` route and the compiler falls back to `immich-local`. Declare two `public` routes and the compile fails — resolve the tie before retrying.
