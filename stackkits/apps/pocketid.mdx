PocketID is the default local identity provider in the released StackKits catalog. A Basement Kit rollout uses it for local owner custody and normally exposes it at `id.<domain>`.

## What it is

PocketID keeps the list of people who may use your homelab and lets them sign in with a passkey, a login method that uses your device instead of a password. A StackKit includes it so that your server has its own sign-in that works inside your home and does not depend on an outside account.

## Which kits include it

The [Basement Kit](/stackkits/kits/basement-kit) page lists local owner custody with PocketID and TinyAuth, and the kit definition marks PocketID as a default platform service.

## How you open it

The catalog gives the pattern `https://id.<domain>`, for example `https://id.home.test` with the default domain. Copy the generated address from the CLI output.

## First use

1. After the rollout verifies successfully, open the generated address.
2. Create the first local owner passkey, following the page's prompts.
3. Keep recovery material outside the server.
4. Return to the [Node Hub](/guides/stackkits/node-hub) and confirm that protected routes ask you to sign in.

## What to expect and what not to change

- Expect a passkey-first setup. Do not use a shared identity for the owner.
- TinyAuth uses the generated PocketID integration for protected StackKits routes. See [TinyAuth](/stackkits/apps/tinyauth) for the entrypoint behavior.
- This account belongs to your own homelab. It is not your kombify Cloud account.
- Do not paste recovery material into a chat, an issue or an e-mail.

## If something goes wrong

- The address does not open: compare it with the address in the rollout output and check that the name resolves on your device. See [Troubleshooting](/guides/stackkits/troubleshooting).
- You cannot sign in to a service: use the account that the owner created for you.
- Run `stackkit verify --json` from the workspace folder and read the findings. Do not reset the owner to get past an error.
