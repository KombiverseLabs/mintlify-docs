Meetings turn a call into a transcript, your own notes, and a cited write-up.
The feature ships in the SpeechKit Windows client from v0.59.0, and from
v0.67.0 Meeting Mode sits beside Dictation, Assist, and Voice Agent in General
Settings with its own settings page for capture, review generation, languages,
retention, and compact-window behavior. Nothing is recorded until you accept,
and meeting audio is never kept.

## Prerequisites

- SpeechKit v0.59.0 or later on Windows 10/11 x64
- a local model or configured provider for transcription and write-ups

## How a meeting starts

SpeechKit notices when a calling application takes the microphone and offers to
take notes. The check reads process names only. It stores nothing and sends
nothing, and it says nothing about who is on the call or what is said.

- Detection covers common clients such as Teams, Zoom, Slack, Discord, and
  Webex, plus browsers, so browser-based calls like Google Meet are included.
- Recording starts only after you accept the offer. Declining a call does not
  cause repeat prompts; the same application is only announced again after its
  microphone has been free for a while.
- You can also start a meeting manually from the overlay control strip. See
  [overlay and keyboard controls](/speechkit/modes#overlay-and-keyboard-controls).

Configure detection in `config.toml`:

```toml
[meeting]
# Turn Meeting Mode on or off (default: true).
enabled = true
# Offer to take notes when a calling app takes the microphone (default: true).
auto_detect = true
# Replace the built-in application list. Empty uses the built-in list.
auto_detect_apps = ["ms-teams.exe", "zoom.exe", "chrome.exe"]
# Write the meeting up as soon as it ends (default: true).
auto_enhance = true
```

## How a meeting ends

From v0.67.7, recordings finish on their own when the call ends. The same
microphone signal that detects a starting call notices when the calling
application has left the microphone, and SpeechKit stops the recording after a
short grace period. Recordings you started by hand without a detected call are
never auto-stopped; finish those yourself. Pause, resume, and finish commands
verify the requested recording session, so one meeting cannot change another
meeting's state.

## While recording: the compact overlay

From v0.67.0, a compact transparent overlay stays within reach while a meeting
records. It shows capture health per channel, elapsed time, and the recording
controls, and an always-visible expand button switches to the full transcript
and notes workspace. The meeting note window closes when the meeting finishes.

```toml
[meeting]
# Start recordings in the compact overlay (default: true).
compact_on_start = true
# Keep the compact overlay above other windows (default: true).
always_on_top = true
```

## Notes during the call

Accepting a meeting opens a meeting notepad window. Notes you type there
autosave while the meeting runs and are stored with the point in the meeting
they were written at. These notes are kept apart from the transcript and from
generated text, and they lead the write-up later.

## Screenshots during a meeting

From v0.67.7, you can capture ad-hoc screen snapshots during a live meeting
with the camera button in the compact overlay and the meeting notepad. Select
the highlighted window to capture it, or press the global `Ctrl+Alt+S` shortcut
to capture the monitor under the cursor immediately.

Snapshots are stored locally with a meeting-timeline timestamp. They appear as
markers in the transcript timeline and in the meeting detail gallery, and
generated Meeting Reviews reference them by their timestamps. Snapshots never
enter model prompts and never leave the machine.

```toml
[meeting]
# Show the screenshot quick action in the meeting UI (default: true).
screenshot_enabled = true
# Global shortcut; "none" disables it (default: "ctrl+alt+s").
screenshot_hotkey = "ctrl+alt+s"
# Arm the global shortcut while a meeting is live (default: true).
screenshot_hotkey_enabled = true
```

## Two tracks, speaker labels for free

A meeting records two separate sources: your microphone and your computer's
system audio output, which is what the other participants are heard through.
Because the channels never mix, the transcript can label your words and the
other side's words without speaker-diarization guesswork. A meeting stays live
when one of its two channels drops.

<Warning>
The system-audio track captures whatever is playing on your machine while the
meeting runs. Recording other people may require their knowledge or consent
where you live; SpeechKit does not judge that for you.
</Warning>

## Privacy and retention

Meetings record people other than you, so they are handled more strictly than
dictation:

- Meeting audio is never saved, regardless of the `save_audio` setting. This is
  enforced in the capture pipeline rather than by a setting, so it cannot be
  switched off. A meeting keeps what was said, not the sound of anyone saying
  it.
- Transcripts and notes are stored locally and kept until you delete them.
- Set `store.meeting_retention_days` to discard finished meetings automatically
  after that many days. Pin an individual meeting to keep it past the sweep.

```toml
[store]
# Discard finished meetings after 30 days. 0 keeps them until you delete them.
meeting_retention_days = 30
```

## The write-up

When the meeting ends, SpeechKit writes it up from the transcript and the notes
you typed, using a template that says what this kind of meeting needs written
down. With `auto_enhance = true` (the default) this happens as soon as the
meeting ends; otherwise you trigger it from the dashboard.

- Your notes are anchors, not raw material. A bullet that came from one of your
  notes keeps your own words verbatim.
- Every generated bullet cites the transcript segments it came from, so you can
  check any claim against what was actually said. A bullet without a citation
  is one the model could not ground.
- Action-item owners and due dates are filled only when the conversation named
  them; they stay empty rather than being guessed.
- A local model can produce the write-up, so meetings do not require an
  external provider.

Read a meeting as its write-up in the dashboard, alongside the transcript and
your hand-written notes.

## Meeting Reviews

From v0.67.0, long meetings produce useful reviews without waiting for the end.
While a meeting records, SpeechKit continuously summarizes bounded transcript
batches without blocking capture. Finishing the meeting produces a durable
Meeting Review, a five-sentence Executive Brief, and action-item candidates,
with retry, cancellation, progress, and provider attribution. SpeechKit shows a
notification when the Meeting Review is ready, or when it failed.

- Reviews support English, German, Spanish, Simplified Chinese, Hindi, and
  Arabic. Reviews in several languages are written one after another rather
  than in parallel, so the bundled local model finishes them reliably.
- Follow-up tasks remain local and link back to their source meeting.

```toml
[meeting]
# Provider that writes reviews: "local" (default) or "github_copilot".
generation_provider = "local"
# What happens when the provider fails (default: "local_only").
fallback_policy = "local_only"
# How many minutes of transcript each rolling summary batch covers (default: 5).
batch_minutes = 5
# Review language; "auto" follows the meeting (default: "auto").
summary_language = "auto"
# Extra languages to write each review in.
additional_summary_languages = ["de"]
```

### Optional cloud generation with GitHub Copilot

Cloud assistance stays optional. GitHub Copilot is available as a desktop
generation provider for Meeting Reviews only after you sign in and grant a
separate, revocable transcript-processing permission. Copilot sessions are
isolated, with no tools and no repository context. The local path remains
available without any cloud credentials.
