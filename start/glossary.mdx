Short explanations of the words you will meet. Each entry says where to read
more.

## Homelab and servers

**Homelab.** One or more computers at home that run services for you and your
household, such as a photo library or a password vault. A mini PC or an old
laptop is enough to start. See [Before you start](/guides/stackkits/before-you-start).

**Server.** A computer that stays on and offers a service to other devices.
The computer can be at home or rented from a hosting company.

**Host.** The computer that a StackKit is installed on.

**Service or application.** A program that does one job for you, for example
Immich for photos or Vaultwarden for passwords. See [Platform services](/stackkits/apps/overview).

**Use case.** Something your household relies on, such as photos or a password
vault. One or more applications provide it. See [Use cases](/guides/stackkits/use-cases/overview).

## StackKits

**StackKits.** The open standard and command-line tool that sets up a homelab
from a short description. See the [overview](/stackkits/overview).

**Kit.** A ready-made starting point. Basement Kit is for hardware at home,
Cloud Kit is for a rented server. See [Choose a kit](/guides/stackkits/choosing-a-kit).

**StackSpec.** The file in which you describe what your stack should contain.
You edit this file, never the generated files. See [Configure a StackSpec](/guides/stackkits/configure-stack-spec).

**Plan.** A preview of what StackKits would change. You read it before you
apply anything. See [Review a plan and apply](/guides/stackkits/review-plan-and-apply).

**Apply.** The step that makes the change on your computer.

**Node Hub.** The overview page of your homelab with links to your services.
See [Node Hub](/guides/stackkits/node-hub).

**Owner.** The person who controls the homelab. The owner approves changes
that alter the stack.

## Addresses and sign-in

**Domain.** The text part of a web address, such as `photos.home`.

**HTTPS.** The secure way a browser talks to a website. The padlock in the
address bar shows it.

**Sign-in with a passkey.** A way to sign in with your fingerprint, face or
device PIN instead of a password. Your local identity service PocketID is built
around passkeys. See [PocketID](/stackkits/apps/pocketid).

**PocketID and TinyAuth.** The two programs that guard your homelab. PocketID
knows who you are. TinyAuth stands in front of services and lets only signed-in
people through. See [How sign-in works](/identity/how-sign-in-works).

**kombify.me.** Public `https://` names for services that run at home. See the
[overview](/kombify-me/overview).

## Accounts

**kombify account.** Your hosted login for kombify Cloud and the AI tools. It
is separate from the owner of your homelab. See [Create an account](/apps/cloud/get-started).

**Household.** A group of people you invite so that you can share things.

## Voice

**Dictation.** Speaking and getting text. **Assist.** One short answer or
rewrite. **Voice Agent.** A spoken conversation. **Meeting.** Recording a call
and getting a written summary. See [Modes](/speechkit/modes).

**Provider.** The service that turns your speech into text. It runs on your
computer or on someone else's.

**Network Scope and Retention Scope.** The two privacy settings of SpeechKit:
where it may connect, and what it keeps. See
[Choose your privacy settings](/speechkit/privacy-settings).

## Backups

**Backup.** A copy you can restore from. **Restore.** Bringing the copy back.
A backup you never tried to restore is not yet a safe backup. See
[Back up and restore](/guides/stackkits/back-up-and-restore).
