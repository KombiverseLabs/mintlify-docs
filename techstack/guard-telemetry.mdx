[kombify Guard](/techstack/kombify-guard) sends only the data classes listed on
this page. A field that is not on the list is not sent, and adding a field means
changing this list first.

<Note>
**Public Beta.** The list is the upper limit of what Guard may send. Not every
Guard version sends every class yet. The retention periods below describe the
Techstack control plane that kombify operates.
</Note>

## What Guard never sends

- Application content or file contents.
- Environment variables, secrets or credentials.
- Personal data of the people in your household.
- The Owner email. It is StackKits configuration, never Guard telemetry.
- The raw machine ID. Guard sends only a digest of it.

## Where the data goes

- **A Techstack you operate yourself.** Guard sends its data only to your own
  Techstack. kombify receives nothing unless you connect the installation.
- **Connected and managed installations.** Guard sends its data to the Techstack
  control plane that kombify operates, over the authenticated Guard channel that
  is bound to the server's own key.
- **Scoped to you.** Telemetry is designed to be scoped to your account, both
  when it is stored and in every interface that reads it. You are not meant to
  see another account's hostnames, addresses, identifiers or metrics.

## What Guard may send

| Data | What it contains | Why | How long it is kept |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Binding | Identifiers of the worker, server, stack, account and owner; a fingerprint of the server's key; the credential generation; the Guard and contract version | Authenticate the server and bind it to you | For as long as the binding lasts. Revocation records are kept 90 days. |
| Liveness | Observation time, sequence numbers, boot ID, a digest of the machine ID, journal health, the number of unresolved outcomes | Show how fresh the connection is and detect restarts and cloned servers | Current state, and the timeline of changes for 90 days |
| Host resources | CPU, memory, swap, load, disk use per mount, uptime, container resource use | Show capacity and health | 15 days |
| Host inventory | Hostname, operating system and version, architecture, CPU, memory and disk size, runtime version, public, private and local IP addresses, SSH host public keys, listening ports | Check reachability, pin the SSH host identity and check what is exposed | Current state. Replaced values are kept for 30 days. |
| Service inventory | StackKit identity, version and mode, service and container names, image references, systemd unit names, probed health, endpoints | Operate and verify services | Current state, and the timeline of changes for 90 days |
| Security posture | The StackKits host-security evidence: the state of each control, expected and observed values, exceptions, baseline version, freshness | Show and alert on the secured configuration | Current state, and findings for 90 days |
| Recovery posture | The age and status of backup snapshots, and the result of the last verified restore drill | Show whether you can recover | Current state, and history for 90 days |
| Operation receipts | Command ID, binding, action, result state and digest, a short result summary, redacted log lines of the executor | Keep a durable, auditable record of what Guard did | Receipts 90 days. Log lines 30 days. |

## During an outage and when you detach

During an outage, Guard stores its data locally within a bounded budget. After
the outage, it either backfills the data or reports an explicit gap.

When you detach or retire a server, Guard deletes its credentials and its local
journal after Techstack acknowledges the handoff. The Techstack control plane
that kombify operates deletes the inventory, metrics and posture of that binding
within 30 days. Audit receipts are the exception, and they follow the periods
above.
