Photos gives your household one library for photos and videos on a host you operate. Immich runs the library, and its mobile apps upload pictures from your phones. The release catalog calls this use case Photos and Memories and delivers it as the optional `photos` workload.

## In plain words

Photos is your own family photo library at home. A program called Immich stores your pictures and videos on your host, and an app on each phone sends new pictures there automatically. It works like a cloud photo service, except that the library is on a computer you control.

## What you need first

- A finished baseline: read [Before you start](/guides/stackkits/before-you-start) and verify your kit.
- Enough free disk space for the library, and a place for backups.
- Phones on the same network for the first upload.
- A decision about which kit hosts it (see below).

## Choose the host

- Use [Basement Kit](/stackkits/kits/basement-kit) for local hardware and LAN-first access.
- Use [Cloud Kit](/stackkits/kits/cloud-kit) for an existing VPS or cloud host with a domain you control.

## Plan storage and recovery

Photos and videos often become the largest data set in a homelab. Plan storage and backups before you import a family library.

- Size primary and backup storage for photo and video growth.
- Recovery needs both the original photos and videos and the Immich database. Either part alone is not a complete restore.
- Practice a staged restore before the library is the only copy of your photos. See [Back up and restore](/guides/stackkits/back-up-and-restore).

## What is stored where

- The original photos and videos are stored on your host, in the persistent allocation the plan creates for the `photos` workload.
- The Immich database, which holds albums, people and settings, is stored separately on the host. Its password is kept in local owner custody, not in the configuration.
- Backups are a separate copy. A restore needs both the original files and the database.

## Set it up

Select the workload and complete first use on the [Immich](/stackkits/apps/immich) page. Then connect each phone with [Back up photos from your phone](/guides/stackkits/use-cases/photos/phone-backup) and check that a test photo arrives.

After each step you should see the following: the plan lists the `photos` workload, a Photos address appears in the Node Hub or the rollout output, you can create the Immich owner account, and a test photo from your phone appears in the library.

## If something goes wrong

- The Photos address does not open: compare it with the address in the Node Hub and see [Troubleshooting](/guides/stackkits/troubleshooting).
- A phone does not upload: check that it resolves your homelab names, as described in [Day to day](/guides/stackkits/daily-use), and follow [Back up photos from your phone](/guides/stackkits/use-cases/photos/phone-backup).
- You are unsure whether your photos are safe: do not treat the library as the only copy until you have practiced a restore in [Back up and restore](/guides/stackkits/back-up-and-restore).
